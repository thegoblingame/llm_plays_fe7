## What actually worked, and how fast

The find took **four calls**, not a diff hunt. The reasoning:

FE7 must copy each combatant somewhere to compute the battle. A copy of a unit struct starts
with the unit's character pointer. So search EWRAM for Raven's character pointer:

```
search_memory(bytes=[0xE8,0xCE,0xBD,0x08], region="EWRAM", align=4)
  -> 0x202BFD8   (his live player-array slot)
     0x203A3F0   (<- this)
```

and the same for the enemy's, which returned all 49 enemy slots **plus `0x203A470`**. The two
extras sit exactly `0x80` apart, which is the whole structure handed over in one shot.

**No snapshot/diff was needed at any point in the primary hunt.** Worth internalising: when
the thing you're looking for is likely to *embed* something you can already address, search
for that instead of diffing.

## Predict-then-read, not diff-then-guess

Before pressing anything I located the **ROM item table** so weapon stats would be inputs
rather than unknowns. Searched all 16 MB of ROM for Iron Sword's stat block
(`2E 05 5A 05 00 11 0A 00` = 46 uses / Mt 5 / Hit 90 / Wt 5 / crit 0 / range 1-1 / cost 10),
got exactly one hit at `0x08BE2264`, which gives `entry(id) = 0x08BE222C + 0x24*id`. Verified
against five consecutive known entries.

That made it possible to compute the entire expected forecast from first principles before
looking at a single forecast byte:

| | predicted | read |
|---|---|---|
| Raven ATK | 20 = Str 13 + Hand Axe Mt 7 | 20 |
| Raven AS | 16 = Spd 19 − (Wt 12 − Con 9) | 16 |
| Raven HIT | 99 = 60 + 19×2 + 3/2 | 99 |
| Raven AVO | 35 = 16×2 + 3 | 35 |
| Raven CRIT | 9 = 0 + 19/2 | 9 |
| Raven DODGE | 3 = Lck | 3 |
| Mage ATK | 15 = 7 + Thunder Mt 8 | 15 |
| Mage HIT | 94 = 80 + 7×2 + 0 | 94 |
| Mage CRIT | 8 = 5 + 7/2 | 8 |
| Raven effHIT | 65 = 99 − 34 | 65 |
| Mage effHIT | 59 = 94 − 35 | 59 |

Two predictions initially "failed" and both were **my prediction being wrong, not the
hypothesis** — the exact trap RAM.md warns about:

- Raven's DEF read 5, not his Def of 9. Correct: the mage attacks with magic, so the field
  holds **Res**. Later re-confirmed by *effect* — backing out to the weapon list flipped it
  straight back to 9.
- The mage's DEF read 4 not 3, and AVO 34 not 14. Correct: `+0x56`/`+0x57` of its own struct
  read `terrainDef 1 / terrainAvo 20`. It is standing in a forest.

## Then confirming semantics by writing, not by more samples

Location was established in four calls; the rest of the session was spent on **meaning**,
because that is where this project's every disproven claim has died.

1. **Wrote Raven's stats** to `Str 20 / Skl 5 / Spd 9 / Res 12 / Lck 0`, backed out and
   re-entered. Every derived field moved exactly as predicted (ATK 27, DEF 12, AS 6, HIT 70,
   AVO 12, CRIT 2, effHIT 36). DEF tracking Res while his Def stayed 9 is the clean proof of
   the def/res selection rule.
2. **Wrote the enemy's Skl/Lck** (7→20, 0→1). HIT 120, AVO 35, CRIT 15, DODGE 1 — all exact.
   This also caught `effHIT` **clamping at 100** (120 − 12 = 108 → 100).
3. **Swapped the weapon** to a Tomahawk (Mt 13 / Hit 65 / Wt 14). Needed an axe-rank write to
   A first, because the weapon list correctly refused a weapon he couldn't use. ATK 33, AS 4,
   HIT 75, AVO 8 — all exact, confirming the ROM item table feeds these fields.
4. **A completely independent second sample**: Hector at `(11,6)` with an Iron Axe against an
   armour knight at `(11,7)`. Different units, different faction weapon types, physical vs
   physical.

## The two things sample 4 turned up

**Weapon triangle, including reavers.** The enemy's lance turned out to be an **Axereaver**
(read by name from `0x0202A5B4`). `+0x53` read `0xE2` = −30 hit and `+0x54` `0xFE` = −2 damage
on Hector, with `+30`/`+2` on the enemy — the reversed, doubled triangle exactly. The plain
axe-vs-anima matchup read 0/0 on both sides.

**Support bonuses are folded into these fields.** Hector read DEF 15 / AVO 31 / CRIT 8 /
DODGE 9 where his base formulas give 14 / 26 / 6 / 4. Rather than assume "supports", I ran a
control: wrote `x = 20` into the five player units within three tiles and re-entered the
forecast. Every one of those four fields dropped to **exactly** the base-formula value.
Raven, who had no allies in range, had matched the bare formulas from the start.

This is the most practically important finding after the addresses themselves: **a tool that
recomputes Atk/Hit/Crit from base stats will be quietly wrong for any unit standing near
friends.** Read the fields.

## The `-4` — a real anomaly, honestly unresolved

The attacker's effective crit is `CRIT − opponent DODGE`. The defender's is
`CRIT − opponent DODGE − 4`. Six readings:

| attacker DODGE | defender CRIT | defender effCRIT | = CRIT − DODGE − 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 8 | 1 | ✓ |
| 0 | 8 | 4 | ✓ |
| 0 | 15 | 11 | ✓ |
| 0 | 8 | 4 | ✓ (different attacker weapon) |
| 9 | 25 | 12 | ✓ (different units entirely) |
| 4 | 25 | 17 | ✓ (same pair, dodge changed by moving allies) |

Ruled out: **not** a support bonus (supports land in `DODGE` itself, which reads exactly
`Lck` for a unit with no partners in range and exactly `Lck + bonus` for one with them);
**not** terrain (both attackers stood on terrain ID 23 with 0/0 bonuses); **not**
weapon-dependent (held across hand axe, tomahawk and iron axe).

**Not resolved: whether the rule is "defender" or "enemy".** Every sample was a player unit
attacking an enemy, so the two readings are indistinguishable. Settling it needs a forecast
captured while an *enemy* is the actor — i.e. during the enemy phase. Logged in RAM.md and
BACKLOG.

## The mistake I nearly shipped

At target select, both `+0x13` (current HP) fields hold post-battle values and both weapon
use-counts are decremented. Raven's read 26 → 16 and the mage's 22 → 0; his hand axe read
20 → 18. It looks exactly like a battle outcome oracle, and it is internally coherent —
17 damage twice kills a 22 HP unit, 10 damage back leaves Raven at 16.

Then it survived a `B` → `A` round trip **byte-identically** (a 512-byte diff over the whole
pair returned **0 changes**), which made it look even more like a real, RNG-settled result.

It is a deterministic **"every blow connects, no criticals"** projection. Two tests:

1. **Forced the attacker's hit rate to zero.** Wrote the mage's Lck to 90 → its AVO 124 →
   Raven's `effHIT` **0**. The projection *still* showed the mage at 0 HP. A 0%-to-hit
   attacker cannot roll two hits.
2. **Committed two real attacks** and compared:

| | projection | actual |
|---|---|---|
| Raven → mage | Raven 26→16, mage 22→**0** | Raven 26→16, mage 22→**22**, axe uses unchanged |
| Hector → armour | Hector 14→7, enemy 15→**0** | Hector 14→7, enemy 15→**4**, axe uses −1 |

The attacker's HP matched both times only because the single enemy counter connected both
times — coincidence, not reliability.

This is the "it reproduces exactly, therefore it's correct" trap in its purest form.
Determinism ruled out randomness and said nothing whatever about meaning. Added to RAM.md's
method notes.

**What the field is actually good for:** `uses(+0x4A) − uses(+0x48)` is the **number of
attacks that side will make** — the doubling indicator — verified on five samples including
a negative one (Tomahawk, AS 4 vs AS 7, showed 1 attack; Hand Axe, AS 16 vs AS 7, showed 2).
And `+0x13` answers "can this kill *at all*": if the projection doesn't reach 0, no sequence
of rolls will.

## Negative results — don't repeat these

- **Target cycling readback is undemonstrated.** `gBattleTarget` definitely identifies the
  *current* target (`+0x0B` roster index `0x91`, `+0x10`/`+0x11` = `(8,7)`, both correct).
  But neither Raven nor Hector had a second enemy in range, so Left/Right cycling was never
  observed to update it. **Manufacturing a second target by writing an enemy's coordinates
  failed** — moved enemy #31 from `(11,8)` to `(10,7)` (write verified, it landed), and the
  target list still contained only the original enemy, with Right doing nothing. FE7 builds
  the attack-target list before the target-select step (at selection / range computation), so
  a late raw position write is not picked up. Next attempt: find a natural two-target
  position, or write the position *before* selecting the attacking unit.

- **The ASCII buffer at `0x0202A5B4` does not follow the action-menu highlight.** It stayed on
  a stale `"Wait"` across several highlight moves in both units' action menus. RAM.md already
  says it shows "the last label rendered"; that is exactly right and it is weaker than it
  sounds. It *is* reliable for the target's weapon name at target select ("Thunder",
  "Axereaver") and for the weapon-info panel header ("Avoid").

- **The action menu is not always 3 entries.** Raven's was 3 (`0x0202532C`), Hector's was 5
  (`0x020254DC`) — a different allocation slot on the same turn, minutes apart. Locate it by
  diff every time, as RAM.md says.

- **Writing an item ID into an inventory slot mid-attack corrupts the inventory.** Overwrote
  Raven's slot 1 while the game had an equipped-slot cached; the subsequent weapon-select
  swap clobbered his Killing Edge entirely. Related and independently useful: **choosing a
  weapon in the attack flow rewrites the live unit's inventory order**, moving the equipped
  weapon into slot 0. Anything that caches inventory offsets across an attack will break.

- **`gBattleActor` is populated as soon as the action menu opens**, but `gBattleTarget` is
  stale garbage from a previous battle until you reach target select. On the first read the
  target block held a dead unit from an earlier enemy-phase fight, which was briefly
  convincing. Gate on `read32(0x0203A474) != 0`.

- **Durability may not be consumed on a miss.** Raven missed every swing and his Hand Axe
  stayed at 20/20; Hector landed 1 of 2 and spent exactly 1. Consistent across both, and
  consistent with RAM.md's earlier −2 observation on a doubling counterattacker that hit
  twice — but it contradicts the usual understanding of FE7 and rests on 2 samples. Tagged
  **Inferred**, logged in RAM.md's open questions.
